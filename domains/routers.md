# Domain guide: Wi-Fi routers and mesh systems

Aliases: router, wifi router, wi-fi router, mesh wifi, mesh system, mesh router

No measurement table: independent, comparable throughput tests are rare and depend heavily on the
home. Products are compared side by side on reviews and on the specs that decide fit.

## Decision factors

- **Home size and layout**: one router for a flat vs a mesh system for multiple floors, thick walls, long homes.
- **Internet plan and ISP**: connection speed; whether the ISP needs PPPoE, VLAN or IPTV settings, or provides its own ONT/router.
- **Wi-Fi standard**: Wi-Fi 6 is the sensible minimum; Wi-Fi 6E/7 only help if the devices support them.
- **Number of devices**: many smart-home devices need a router that handles many connections.
- **Wired ports**: WAN port speed (2.5 GbE for 1 Gbps+ plans), LAN ports for PCs, TVs, consoles.
- **Mesh backhaul**: wired (Ethernet) backhaul is far better than wireless if cables are possible.
- **Software**: app vs web interface, parental controls, guest network, VPN, QoS.
- **Security updates**: how long the maker provides firmware updates.

## What actually matters

- **Placement and walls matter more than headline speeds** ("AX6000" adds up all bands and is never reached by one device).
- **Mesh beats a single powerful router** for multi-storey or long homes.
- **Firmware support** keeps the network secure; cheap models are often abandoned quickly.

## Sources

- In-depth reviewers who test throughput at distance (e.g. Dong Knows Tech).
- RTINGS where available.
- Owner communities (Reddit r/HomeNetworking, regional ISP forums) for ISP compatibility and firmware stability.

## Pitfalls

- **Combined speed numbers** (AX3000, BE9300) are marketing totals, not real throughput.
- **ISP routers** may need to be put in bridge mode to use a new router properly.
- **Wi-Fi 7 benefits** need Wi-Fi 7 devices; otherwise the gain is small.
